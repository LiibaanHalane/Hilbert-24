# Native torser involutions and polylogarithmic H24

This extends the previous UltrametricH24 development, retaining its original source and minimum receiver. The new matrix program uses exactly the native sparse chart `rho(t,b,p,h,r,k)` and the original Gram G. No global SAT norm is assumed computable. The appended Lean transport declarations are supplied for replay, not claimed kernel-checked.

## 1. Native algebra and isometries

rho(t,b,B) has boundary block [[t-2b,0],[b,t]] and middle block B. The native algebra is closed under matrix addition and multiplication, and under G-dagger, with G boundary block [[1,1],[1,2]] and middle H=[[2,-1],[-1,2]]. Both G and G^-1 are integral over Z_2. The entry maximum norm is submultiplicative by the strong triangle inequality; rho and its coefficient decoder both have norm at most one, hence preserve the norm. The dagger is isometric by applying its own inverse. N_D=-(I+2D)/3 and L=I-2D have integral coefficients and are inverse to one another. They too preserve norm. This is a norm statement, not positivity of a 2-adic inner product.

For the odd order-two Lerch chart tau(A)=sum_j (-4)^j A^(2j+1)/(2j+1)^2, the power difference telescope A^r-B^r=sum_i A^i(A-B)B^(r-1-i) proves that tau-id is 1/4-Lipschitz. Hence tau is an isometry by the strict ultrametric inequality. The recursion X_next=Y+X-tau(X) constructs the inverse through each finite cut. Since powers commute with taking dagger, tau(A^dagger)=tau(A)^dagger. The original coefficientwise chart is separately transported by its decoder; it need not commute with the untransported coordinate dagger.

## 2. Original clause involutions

Let f_j(a) be the original failed-clause bit, P the middle-block identity embedded with zero anchors, and U_j=I-2f_j(a)P. These are native G-self-adjoint involutions. For c=tau(1), an odd 2-adic unit, tau(U_j)=c U_j, since every odd power of U_j equals U_j. Therefore (3c I-tau(U_j))/(2c)=I+f_j(a)P. The numerator is divisible by 2; computing it one extra bit before dividing gives the exact factor modulo 2^k. The product over original labels is rho(1,0,2^A_C(a) I_2). Do not measure the full matrix norm, which is always one because of the anchors. Subtract I-P to isolate the middle block. One bit of the marked product is the original satisfaction bit. The product is local to one supplied assignment, not the maximum over all assignments.

## 3. Genuine nonlinear involution (archived Landen chart)

On the same native integral matrix algebra define K(A)=A(I+2A)^-1(I+6A)^-1 and M(A)=Li_2(16K(A))/16. Invert the denominators by convergent geometric series. K(A)=A+4H(A) for integral restricted power series H, since the denominator equals I+8A+12A^2. The nth coefficient of the normalized dilog in K is 16^(n-1)/n^2, of valuation at least 2(n-1). Thus M(A)=A+4H'(A), with H' nonexpansive, and M is an isometry with an explicit inverse from the same contraction. It commutes with dagger. All matrix identities occur in the commutative subalgebra generated by a single A; no commutativity of arbitrary A,B is assumed.

Writing g=16K, the original rational identity is g(-A)=g(A)(g(A)-I)^-1. The formal Landen identity Li_2(g)+Li_2(g/(g-1))=-(log(1-g))^2/2 holds by differentiating the scalar power series and taking the value at zero. Substitution converges in the native algebra. Let ell(A)=log(I-g(A))/16, an integral restricted series. It gives M(-A)=-M(A)-8 ell(A)^2. Thus J(Y)=M(-M^-1(Y)) is an explicit involution, with its analytic expression -Y-8 ell(M^-1(Y))^2. In particular J^2=id; J fixes Y iff Y=0, since 2A=0 iff A=0 over Z_2. At finite cut k, fixedness only proves A=0 mod 2^(k-1). This guard is necessary. For Y=M(A), H(Y)=(Y-J(Y))/2 satisfies v_2(H(Y))=v_2(A) since M is an isometry and A-(-A)=2A. Use the half displacement at k+1 bits to return k bits, or evaluate the odd part directly. The old odd Lerch chart conjugates sign just to sign, so it alone does not give a new nonlinear sign involution. M does. The half displacement is a norm receiver, not an unproved global evaluation algorithm.

## 4. Ordinary native self-adjoint involution carrying a coefficient

For v=(1,u)^T and H=[[2,-1],[-1,2]], put d=1-u+u^2 (always odd), R(u)=I_2-v v^T H/d. Since v^T H v=2d, the rank-one term T satisfies T^2=2T, so R^2=I and R^dagger_H=R. The entry R_12=(1-2u)/d is a unit, and u=(R_22-1)/R_12. Both the encode and this inverse preserve congruences, proving the exact isometry v_2(R(u)-R(v))=v_2(u-v). Embed with rho(1,0,R(u)), at most six nonzeros. This produces ordinary Gram-self-adjoint involutions, not just an abstract conjugated involution. For a grade u=2^e, compare R(tau(u)) with R(0): their difference has valuation e. An involution by itself is a unit; its norm is not the grade. Its marked displacement is.

## 5. Two branches and H24

Place two acquired grade monomials u,v in the middle diagonal with zero anchors. Both tau and M preserve this subspace and act separately on the two diagonal entries. The native involution S=rho(1,0,[[0,1],[1,0]]) is Gram-self-adjoint and conjugates the diagonal by swapping its entries. It also intertwines both series charts and their half-displacement. Read the two first occupied digits (or the two displacement norms); select the larger norm and retain both flags at a tie, with source address. This is the original UltrametricH24.choose operation. At an all-zero finite cut report unobserved unless a grade bound proves emptiness. It is not ordinary matrix addition: equal valuations can cancel. All old H24 tight pin and witness return theorems then apply. The global norm of the family over all assignments remains a separate computation.

For fixed native dimension and precision k, the geometric series, odd inverses and restricted series evaluations take O(k^3) schoolbook bit operations; inverse charts use O(k) updates, O(k^4). The one-guard-bit defect does not change those polynomial bounds. A supplied assignment can be evaluated in polynomial bit time, including reading its clause labels. Neither the analytic coordinate change nor an integral involution proves that acquiring the two global minimum signals costs polynomial time in the succinct unrestricted CNF.

## 6. Replay

Run `python3 check_native_involution.py` (standard library). It checks the actual sparse matrices and exact finite cuts, including direct and inverse charts, the two daggers, Landen, half-displacement, reflection, clause factors and two-branch swaps. `checks.json` is its actual run result. The general proofs are the arguments above; finite checks are not substitutes for these. `NativeInvolutionH24.lean` is the previous standalone source plus `NativeInvolutionExtension.inc`. To replay the Lean source use `lean NativeInvolutionH24.lean`. Lean, Hets and Agda executables were not available in the current environment; the appended propositions are not presented as newly checked kernel results. The finite-cut isometry is supplied to the generic Lean transport theorem as an explicit hypothesis; the restricted-series proof is above.
